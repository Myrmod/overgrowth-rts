# Lore Canon

This page is the current shared lore spine for Overgrowth. Use it as the reference point when writing faction, economy, map, and PvE mission documentation.

## Core Pitch

Earth is not being invaded by a random alien army or overrun by ordinary plants. Humanity accidentally awakened an ancient Amuns bioforming system buried beneath anchor sites across the planet. The system was designed to prepare Earth for eventual Amuns reclamation, but the activation happened without Amun calibration. The result is a broken world where energy, ecology, faith, survival, and sovereignty are all tied to the same living Vine network.

PvE should focus on the consequences of that broken system: dangerous wild zones, failed experiments, dormant Amuns defenses, refugee survival, hostile ecosystems, and missions where every faction sees the crisis through a different moral lens.

## Timeline

- Prehistory: The Amuns govern Earth from behind myth, seed the Vines, and build pyramids, temples and monuments as dimensional anchors.
- Withdrawal: The Amuns leave Earth in a veil state, expecting the dormant system to grow and remain controllable until they return.
- 2030: The Ash War shatters the global order and leaves humanity dependent on unstable power hubs.
- 2069: Researchers seeking a new energy source discover a dormant Vine organism under an ancient Egyptian anchor site.
- 2069: The Awakening begins when desperate governments over-amplify the system. The Vines activate globally without Amuns control.
- 2070-2074: Emergency Vine regimes keep surviving cities powered while refugee corridors, burn zones, exclusion zones, and uncontrolled growth reshape the world.
- 2075-2082: The human schism hardens. Pro-Vine authorities become the Legion, anti-conversion settlements become the Remnants, the Radix coheres, and the Wild spreads.
- Late 2080s: The Current War begins with mature human ideologies, an organized Radix, returning Amuns, and a world where the Wild is a standing PvE threat.

## Human Collapse and Awakening

By the late 2020s, humanity was halfway through an energy transition. Fossil fuel investment had been slashed, green infrastructure had not scaled fast enough, rare earth exports were weaponized, and global grids were increasingly unstable. In 2029, a naval confrontation near a major maritime chokepoint destroyed a tanker and triggered global panic. Energy prices surged, blackouts spread, desalination failed, and supply chains broke down.

Several powers began treating energy infrastructure as legitimate military targets. Precision strikes hit LNG terminals, nuclear facilities, and strategic grid nodes. A missile strike near an early-warning radar was misread as preparation for nuclear decapitation. A limited de-escalatory strike followed, then retaliation within minutes. By 2030, the Ash War had not erased humanity, but it had dismantled the world order. Civilization survived as isolated power hubs, irradiated wastelands, fortified cities, and fractured states built around whatever energy remained.

From 2030 to 2069, the Long Blackout made reconstruction almost impossible. Regional blackouts became permanent, fossil reserves were nearly depleted, fusion could not scale, and atmospheric dust reduced solar output. Energy became currency, political authority, and military leverage.

In 2069, research teams searching for clean energy found a dormant Vine organism beneath an ancient Egyptian anchor site. One scientist warned that the organism was alive and should not be stimulated with artificial power. Governments ignored the warning, imprisoned the scientist for spreading panic, and over-amplified the organism because the alternative looked like total blackout. The anchor network answered.

Energy pulses spiked in Egypt. Dormant spores activated within hours, continental growth events followed within weeks, and within a month overgrowth had become a global crisis. The Vines swallowed infrastructure, destroyed farms, reshaped cities, and mutated human and animal populations. At the same time, energy output surged. For some, the Vines looked like salvation. For others, they were invasion.

The pro-Vine bloc began as ministries, officers, researchers, corporate authorities, and emergency coalitions trying to keep the lights on. In the early 2070s, their rule was mostly triage: protect Vine reactors, suppress panic, ration energy, and keep surviving cities from falling back into the Long Blackout. They suppressed disaster reports because losing access to Vine energy meant returning to collapse.

Through the later 2070s, survival policy hardened into doctrine. Reactor security became shrine defense. Medical stabilization became controlled hybridization. Emergency obedience became moral discipline. By the early 2080s, the pro-Vine bloc had become the Legion.

The Remnants came from those outside that decision-making structure: refugees, deserters, local militias, engineers, and settlements that saw the Awakening firsthand. They sided with the imprisoned scientist's warning because they had never been asked to consent to mutation, conversion, or sacrifice. During the 2070s, scattered camps and burn crews became mutual-defense compacts, salvage networks, and settlement militias. By the early 2080s, those networks had become the Remnants.

The same formation period let the Vines change from a global disaster into several recognizable forces. Adaptive growth became the organized Radix, while damaged, isolated, or corrupted outbreaks became the Wild. By the late 2080s, every faction in the Current War has had enough time to become an ideology rather than an immediate panic response.

Human-focused PvE missions should make this split concrete through civilian evacuations, Vine reactor zones, pre-Awakening archives, refugee corridors, hybridization sites, and contaminated infrastructure that must be burned, captured, or stabilized.
